## Why This Page Exists

This module uses MATLAB because many broadcast engineering ideas are easier to
understand when you can see them changing on screen.

The aim is not to turn every topic into programming. The aim is to make signals,
noise, transmission, and balancing easier to picture.

## Why This Matters

Students often learn the vocabulary before they fully understand the mechanism.
MATLAB helps close that gap by showing:

- how waveforms change
- how channels damage signals
- how noise affects decisions
- why balanced systems reject interference
- how modulation changes both time and frequency views

## The Teaching Rule

For each difficult concept, try to give:

1. one plain-language explanation
2. one static diagram or equation
3. one MATLAB visualisation
4. one short interpretation task

## What We Will Use It For

- signals, channels, and bandwidth
- noise and SNR
- transmission media and line effects
- modulation and multiplexing
- balanced audio and interference rejection

## How To Read The Demos

When you see a MATLAB demo in the wiki, ask:

- what are students likely to misunderstand?
- what can MATLAB show in under two minutes?
- what output should students interpret rather than just watch?
