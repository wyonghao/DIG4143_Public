# DIG4143 – Week 1
## Foundations of Information in Digital Media Systems

---

## Slide 1 – Module Framing

### Week Objectives
- Establish an engineering perspective on information
- Distinguish between data, signal, and information
- Understand why “information” is central to digital media systems
- Build conceptual foundations for Week 2 (Information Theory)

---

## Slide 2 – What Is Information?

### Intuitive Definition
Information = Reduction of uncertainty

### Engineering Perspective
- Information is not “content”
- Information is structured difference
- Information must be:
  - Representable
  - Encodable
  - Transmittable
  - Recoverable

### Discussion Prompt
- Does a pure white image contain information?
- Is noise a form of information?

---

## Slide 3 – Data vs Signal vs Information

| Level | Definition | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| Data | Raw symbols or numbers | 101101 |
| Signal | Physical carrier | Voltage waveform |
| Information | Probabilistic difference | Rare event |

### Key Distinction
- Data ≠ Information
- Signal ≠ Meaning
- Information depends on probability structure

---

## Slide 4 – Digital Representation

### Why Can Everything Be Digitised?
- Binary systems are stable
- Easier error detection and correction
- Efficient compression
- Scalable transmission

### Core Transformation
Continuous world → Sampling → Quantisation → Encoding

---

## Slide 5 – Sampling and Quantisation (Conceptual Layer)

### Sampling
- Discretisation in time
- Sampling rate limits recoverable information

### Quantisation
- Discretisation in amplitude
- Bit depth determines precision

### Critical Questions
- When does information loss occur?
- How do engineers balance cost and quality?

---

## Slide 6 – Systems Thinking in Media Engineering

A digital media system typically includes:
1. Source
2. Encoder
3. Channel
4. Decoder
5. Receiver

This is the engineering precursor to the Shannon communication model.

---

## Slide 7 – Noise and Imperfection

### Sources of Noise
- Physical interference
- Quantisation error
- Compression artefacts
- Network packet loss

### Core Engineering Question
How can a system remain reliable under imperfect conditions?

---

## Slide 8 – Why Information Theory Matters

### Within This Module
- Guides compression algorithms
- Determines bandwidth requirements
- Shapes broadcast architectures
- Defines theoretical system limits

### Central Insight
Engineering systems are mechanisms for managing uncertainty.

---

## Slide 9 – Critical Perspective

### Technology Is Not Neutral
- Encoding strategies influence visibility
- Compression affects perceptual quality
- Algorithms shape distribution fairness

Discussion:
- Who decides what gets prioritised in transmission?
- Can bandwidth limitations produce informational inequality?

---

## Slide 10 – Week 1 Summary

By the end of this week, students should understand:

- Information is not the same as data
- Information has a probabilistic structure
- Digital systems rely on discretisation
- Media engineering is fundamentally about uncertainty management
- Week 2 formalises these ideas using entropy
